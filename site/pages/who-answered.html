The distinction everything else hangs on

Three different things are called “AI provenance”

Two are covered by standards, tooling and now regulation. The third — the one that decides whether the other two mean anything at the point of use — has none.

Covered

Artifact provenance

What went into the model — datasets, code, weights, licences, training compute.

Instruments: CycloneDX ML-BOM v1.7 · SPDX 3.0 AI & Dataset profiles · OpenSSF Model Signing + Sigstore/Rekor · NIST SP 800-218A PS.2.1 · G7/CISA SBOM for AI — Minimum Elements (14 May 2026, explicitly non-mandatory).

Stops at: artifacts at rest. A signed model on a hub says nothing about an inference endpoint. Only <2% of Hugging Face commits are signed at all.
Covered

Content provenance

Was this output machine-generated, and how was it edited?

Instruments: C2PA / Content Credentials 2.4 · EU AI Act Article 50 machine-readable marking of synthetic content, applicable since 2 Aug 2026 (grace to 2 Dec 2026).

Stops at: a compliant marking says “this was AI-generated.” It does not say by what. It is also media-oriented and does not attach usefully to LLM text — the dominant commercial modality.
Uncovered

Runtime provenance

Which weights, at which numerical precision, operated by whom, in which jurisdiction, produced this response?

Instruments: TEE attestation — provider opt-in, and no CPU or GPU attestation in existence carries a claim about the model. Statistical black-box audits — probabilistic, reference-dependent, and now demonstrably spoofable. Client-side fingerprinting — slides 21–23.

Status: no standard, no regulation, no retention duty for routing decisions, and no enforcement action anywhere alleging an API served model B while billing for model A.

What the routers optimise for

  • Snowflake Cortex — “enterprises can select ‘auto’ instead of a fixed model, and the system routes each task to whichever model offers the best combination of quality and cost.” Claims up to 3× token-cost reduction.
  • GitHub HydraFusion — measured against Opus 5: TerminalBench 2.1 +4.9pp at 67% lower cost; DeepSWE −1.5pp at 36% lower; CheckpointBench −0.1pp at 65% lower. VentureBeat's own headline: it “only matches quality in one.”

  • OpenRouter Auto Exacto — reorders providers on every tool-calling request from live telemetry. On by default; opt out with sort: "price" or by pinning.

Why a federal audience should care first

FY2026 NDAA §1532 requires DoD to exclude DeepSeek and High-Flyer by name within 30 days. §6604 requires the DNI to remove DeepSeek from IC systems within 60 days.

Both are entity prohibitions. Neither creates any obligation on a vendor to disclose what model is serving a request, and neither creates any capability to detect a prohibited model arriving through an intermediary, a reseller, a router, or a distilled derivative.

A prohibition that cannot be verified at the point of consumption is the cleanest statement of the gap available in US law.

Steelman · the four strongest objections

What cuts against this case

Every one of these will be raised. Raising them first is cheaper than being corrected.

1  “Precision is a bad proxy for quality”

Moustafa, 7 Sep 2026, measured directly: “The fp4 hosts land in the middle of the fp8 pack… precision is a bad proxy for quality.”

Concede it. A rule mandating quantization labels alone would not predict quality and would create false assurance. The finding argues for disclosing identity and serving configuration, not for precision labels as a standalone remedy.

2  Legitimate variation is genuinely large

CISPA’s 16.6 percentage points is the confound that breaks every behavioural detection method, including Model Equality Testing and IRIS.

Concede it. A measured distributional difference may be an honest engine upgrade. No published method separates honest backend variation from substitution cleanly. This is why the answer is a disclosure duty backed by measurement — not measurement alone.

3  Fingerprints can be deliberately faked

GhostPrint (Penn State, June 2026) fine-tunes a weaker model to mimic a stronger model’s fingerprint, defeating LLMmap, Model Equality Testing and LLM-idiosyncrasies by name.

Concede it. Black-box verification is an arms race with a cost asymmetry favouring the provider. Slide 23 deals with this properly — no slide in this deck claims detection is settled.

4  A federal advisory cuts the other way

AA26-251A (8 Sep 2026) recommends providers “deploy targeted response changes that subtly alter responses to suspected malicious distillation attempts.”

This is a real tension, not a gotcha. A US advisory endorses undisclosed per-account response differentiation for an anti-extraction purpose, eight days after the EU asked 30+ providers about transparency. If a per-request identity duty is written, the counter-distillation carve-out has to be explicit, narrow and logged — not left implicit for every provider to interpret.

The single most quotable finding

One provider’s stable public identifier covered “10 releases / 19 months” — name fixed, served artifact swapped ten times.

Every benchmark result, safety assessment, procurement attestation and academic citation referencing that identifier during those nineteen months refers to something that changed underneath it, without notice, ten times over.

Why this indicts the disclosure model itself

Three of nine providers permit publishing benchmark results at all; the rest restrict it through terms of service — so independent verification is contractually constrained even where it is technically possible.

The authors’ framing is the one to carry into a rulemaking: the industry has produced transparency without verifiability. Model cards, system cards, framework publications and technical documentation all exist. None of them is checkable against what a customer receives.

A procurement instrument · four tiers of attested inference

Almost the whole market sells Tier 1 and markets it as Tier 2

When a vendor claims “attested inference,” this is the question to ask. Select a tier.

Hardware attestation proves the box is honest. It does not prove the box contains what the label says. No CPU or GPU attestation in existence carries a claim about the model. Everything above the boot image is the deployer’s problem — and almost no deployer solves it.

What the silicon actually attests

NVIDIA’s attestation has 31 claims. None is about the model.

The gap between what confidential computing proves and what buyers believe it proves is the widest in this deck.

NVIDIA H100 / H200 / Blackwell

Proves: genuine Hopper or Blackwell silicon, confidential-compute mode active, secure boot on, debug off, running exactly the VBIOS and driver versions NVIDIA has published reference measurements for.

Does not prove: which weights were loaded, which model served the request, what precision was used, or anything about userspace. The detached EAT claims schema v3.0 has 31 claims covering certificates, signatures, firmware measurements and system state — and zero workload claims.

Intel TDX + Trust Authority

Proves: a genuine Trust Domain on genuine patched silicon, launched from a measured image (MRTD), with measured subsequent events (RTMR0-3), and 32 caller-chosen bytes bound into the quote via report_data.

Does not prove: MRTD is a launch measurement — anything loaded later is invisible unless explicitly extended into an RTMR. Intel’s IMA-to-RTMR path is the only documented route for a weights file to reach a quote, and Intel’s own paper on it measures system binaries, not weights. Trust Domain Integrity is explicitly scoped to virtual BIOS and kernel.

AMD SEV-SNP

Proves: a genuine SEV-SNP guest launched from a specific measured image, on a specific chip, at a specific TCB level, with 64 caller-chosen bytes in REPORT_DATA.

Does not prove: post-launch state. SEV-SNP has no runtime measurement registers analogous to TDX RTMRs, so anything loaded after launch — i.e. the weights — sits outside MEASUREMENT unless the guest implements its own measured-load discipline.

The one shipping implementation — and what it exposes

Verification works exactly where the incentive to cheat is lowest

One company proves which model is running. The mechanism is sound, replicable, and structurally unavailable to closed frontier models.

Tinfoil · “Modelwrap” · 3 Feb 2026 · Tier 2/3

How to actually prove which weights are loaded

  1. Model weights hashed into a Merkle tree; the 32-byte root hash is the commitment.
  2. The root hash is passed on the kernel command line, which is covered by the enclave measurement.
  3. dm-verity intercepts every disk read and verifies the block against the attested root — “throwing an error if even a single bit of the disk does not correspond to the committed weights.”
  4. Client verifies the hardware-signed attestation to the CPU vendor root, compares measurements against a Sigstore bundle published by the GitHub Actions build, and establishes TLS bound to the attested public key.

AMD SEV-SNP CPUs plus NVIDIA Hopper/Blackwell in CC mode. Explicitly does not protect against network metadata, hardware implementation flaws, physical attacks, side channels or client compromise — and requires trusting AMD, Intel, NVIDIA, Tinfoil’s GitHub org and Sigstore.

The closed-weight paradox

For a model whose weights nobody outside the lab can obtain, a published hash proves only self-consistency. It detects a silent swap between published versions. It cannot prove the weights are the advertised model, because there is no independent reference to compare against.

The same limitation ends Model Equality Testing and IRIS: both need a reference distribution, which exists only for open weights.

The consequence nobody has drawn

Weight openness is a precondition for runtime verifiability.

Stanford’s open-foundation-models work argues open weights should be assessed on marginal risk. Neither that paper nor the FMTI makes this argument — that openness is what makes independent verification possible at all. And the commercial sting: the only configuration where verification fully works is the one where the incentive to substitute is lowest.

An instrument for the gap · provenance-probe

Measure the endpoint you are already paying for

Open source, local execution, no vendor cooperation, no API key required for the strongest signal. Five independent layers combined by log-odds into two separate verdicts.

The safety property

“When the strongest layer returns nothing, the verdict floors at INDETERMINATE — never a false clean bill.”

An endpoint that suppresses token counts yields a degraded-confidence assessment from wire and latency alone. It cannot be manoeuvred into producing an unearned all-clear — a hard-evidence floor means a model’s own self-report can never drive the verdict — which is what makes it usable as evidence rather than as marketing.

The counterweight · error bars first

Fingerprints can be deliberately faked

Published three months ago. Any slide claiming behavioural detection is settled is now wrong, including the one before this.

GhostPrint · Penn State · 15 Jun 2026 · preprint

“Your ‘Pro’ LLM Subscription May Actually Be ‘Free’”

Introduces fingerprint spoofing: a provider serves a weaker model that has been parameter-efficiently fine-tuned to mimic the stronger model’s fingerprint — surrogate modelling, reward-ranked fine-tuning and distillation — defeating user-side verification.

It explicitly targets and defeats LLMmap, Model Equality Testing and LLM-idiosyncrasies by name — Stanford’s ICLR 2025 paper and the ICML 2025 paper among them.

The authors concede universal spoofing is mathematically impossible under infinite resources — and prove it succeeds under finite query budgets and weak classifiers, which describes every real audit. No attack success rates or named commercial providers are disclosed; preprint, not peer-reviewed.

What this actually means

Black-box behavioural verification is an arms race with a cost asymmetry favouring the provider — the same information asymmetry UC Berkeley named in 2025, now demonstrated constructively.

Add the confound from CISPA: a measured difference may be an honest backend change. And the negative result from August 2026 showing token-count similarity failing on holdout — sensitivity 0.50 — a caution against cheap proxies. The field is self-correcting in public, which is a good sign about the field and a bad sign about relying on detection alone.

And why that strengthens the policy case

The answer is not “build a better detector.”

Detection alone loses an arms race against a well-resourced provider. Detection plus a legal obligation to have recorded the truth does not — because then spoofing a fingerprint is not a clever engineering result, it is falsification of a required record. Measurement makes a disclosure duty enforceable; the duty is what makes measurement matter.

Error bars, stated first

What this cannot do

An instrument presented without its limits is advocacy, not measurement.

Evidence, not proof

Tokenizer and behavioural fingerprinting yield probabilistic verdicts with an explicit confidence label. That supports an investigation, a contractual audit right, or a regulatory information request. It does not by itself sustain a finding of fraud and must not be presented as if it did.

Spoofable in principle

GhostPrint demonstrates that a provider willing to fine-tune can mimic a target fingerprint under finite query budgets. Detection is an arms race with a cost asymmetry favouring the provider. This is the strongest single argument against relying on measurement alone — and for pairing it with a legal record.

Degrades under suppression

Full confidence requires the endpoint to report token usage. Endpoints that suppress token counts force a degraded assessment on wire and latency alone. A determined adversary can raise the cost of measurement — at the price of removing the usage transparency its own customers bill against.

Passive observation has a ceiling

Trace-only assessment through the sentinel proxy cannot run active tokenizer probes, so it floors at INDETERMINATE. Reaching CONFIRMED requires active probing of the backend — which requires authorisation.

Probing is gated, and must be

Targets carry an authorized flag and active probing aborts without it. Behavioural probes transmit politically sensitive prompts and are separately gated. API keys stay in memory and never enter report files. An unauthorised probing regime would be its own legal problem.

Attribution is bounded

A tokenizer match identifies a family, not a specific checkpoint, fine-tune or numerical precision. Latency is corroborating only and never decides. Catalogue provenance entries are labelled “sub-confirmed pointers,” not measurements.

The correct claim: an independent instrument does not replace a disclosure duty. It is what makes a disclosure duty enforceable — the difference between regulating self-report and regulating a fact.

In one sentence

We regulate what a model is, and what it produces.
Nobody measures which model answered.

Safety evaluation, origin restriction, express warranty, evidentiary authentication, a named entity ban in the FY2026 NDAA and the entire GPAI transparency regime all rest on a fact that no instrument currently establishes and no law currently requires anyone to record. The gap is closable — the instruments are open source and running tonight. What is missing is the requirement.

Require the record

Per-request resolved model identity, precision and serving jurisdiction as a response field. Twelve-month retention of routing decisions. The NIST signing duty already exists — bind it to a served response.

Protect the measurers

A safe harbour for good-faith independent verification, so a disclosure duty has auditors capable of testing it. Every measurement in this deck was produced without one.
